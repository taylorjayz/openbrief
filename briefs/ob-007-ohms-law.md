Ohm’s law relates voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) in many conductive materials: V = I × R. Raise resistance and, for a fixed voltage, current falls; raise voltage across a fixed resistance and current rises.

That single relationship unlocks resistor sizing, LED current limiting, and a first intuition for why thin wires heat up under high current. It is a model with limits—real devices can be nonlinear—but it is the right first tool on the bench.

Electronics habit: before blaming a microcontroller, measure whether the power rail and series resistances make physical sense. Math first, mysteries second.
